Selected work — Service design

Quality at scale.

Building better content standards for a more consistent customer experience — not through a single interface, but through the documentation, requirements and processes behind it.

Discipline
Service design
Focus
Content quality & accessibility
Deliverables
Standards, QA, documentation
Status
Ongoing

01 — Overview

Consistency is an
ongoing challenge.

When thousands of businesses are presented through the same platform, maintaining a consistent level of quality becomes an ongoing challenge. Companies deliver logos, images, text and other marketing materials with widely varying levels of quality. Small issues early in the process can affect everything from production workflows to the final experience visitors encounter online.

As part of my role, I have contributed to improving documentation, material requirements and quality standards that help create more consistent customer-facing experiences.

This work is still evolving and represents an ongoing effort to strengthen quality throughout the content journey.

02 — The challenge

Bigger than any
single profile.

Businesses differ in:

Digital maturity Design knowledge Image quality Content quality Understanding of platform requirements

Without clear expectations, these variations can lead to:

  • 01Inconsistent visual presentation
  • 02Additional production work
  • 03Delays in delivery
  • 04Accessibility issues
  • 05Reduced trust in published content

The challenge was therefore larger than individual designs or profiles. It was about improving the system that supports quality across many different customer journeys.

03 — The goal

A more scalable
approach to quality.

Create clearer expectations for customer-delivered materials and contribute to a more scalable approach to quality. The ambition was to support:

  • 01More consistent content quality
  • 02Better customer guidance
  • 03Reduced friction between teams
  • 04Improved accessibility and readability
  • 05Stronger end-user experiences

04 — My role

Five kinds of quiet work.

My contribution has focused on helping define and communicate quality standards throughout the production process.

Documentation

Creating and improving internal documentation that helps teams understand quality expectations.

Material requirements

Contributing to clearer requirements for customer-delivered assets such as logos, images and content.

Quality assurance

Reviewing material and identifying opportunities to improve clarity, consistency and presentation.

Information structure

Organising information in ways that make guidelines easier to understand and apply.

Accessibility awareness

Promoting good practices related to readability, visual quality and information hierarchy.

05 — Understanding the service

Quality is created long
before publication.

One of the most important lessons from this work is that quality is created long before content is published. If expectations are unclear at the beginning of the process, challenges often appear later. By improving requirements and communication upstream, it becomes easier to improve quality downstream.

06 — Design principles

Five things the work
continues to hold to.

  • 01

    Clarity

    Requirements should be easy to understand and communicate.

  • 02

    Consistency

    Customers should be represented through a common quality framework.

  • 03

    Accessibility

    Content should remain understandable and usable for a broad audience.

  • 04

    Efficiency

    Reducing uncertainty helps create smoother workflows.

  • 05

    Trust

    Higher quality content contributes to a more trustworthy experience.

07 — Current progress

Not a destination —
a process.

The initiative has already contributed to better foundations for consistency and quality, but there is still significant potential for improvement.

The work remains ongoing and continues to evolve alongside changing customer needs, production requirements and internal processes.

Rather than viewing quality as a fixed destination, I see it as a continuous process of refinement.

08 — Looking ahead

Quality thinking,
earlier in the journey.

An area I am particularly interested in developing further is how quality thinking can be introduced earlier in the customer journey. Future opportunities include:

  • 01Better onboarding practices
  • 02Stronger internal knowledge sharing
  • 03More effective sales enablement
  • 04Improved customer guidance
  • 05Further accessibility considerations

By helping teams understand quality expectations earlier, it becomes easier to build better experiences from the beginning.

09 — Reflection

“Good user experiences are often created through systems, standards and processes that remain invisible to end users.”

While visitors see a finished profile, the quality of that experience is influenced by countless decisions made long before publication. For me, this work represents an ongoing exploration of how structure, documentation and shared standards can contribute to better experiences at scale.
